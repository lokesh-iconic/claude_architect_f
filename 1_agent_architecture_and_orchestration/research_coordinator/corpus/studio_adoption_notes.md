# Studio adoption notes

Date: 2025-04-16
Source: internal practitioner interviews (anonymised), sample of 14 studios

Generative tooling has entered production pipelines unevenly, and the pattern
is consistent enough across the sample to be worth stating plainly. Adoption
concentrates in the pre-production phase, where output is discarded anyway:
mood boards, storyboards, previsualisation passes and layout blocking. Teams
described this as low-risk because nothing produced at that stage ships.

Final-frame work remains overwhelmingly human-authored across the sample. The
reasons given were practical rather than ideological: art direction notes are
hard to apply to generated output, revisions are difficult to target, and
rights provenance for delivered assets has to be defensible to the client.

Several studios reported a second-order effect on team shape. Where
previsualisation used to absorb junior artists for weeks, that work now
compresses into days, and the junior pipeline that fed senior roles has
narrowed. Two studios described this as their most serious medium-term
concern, ahead of any direct cost question.

Review overhead grew rather than shrank. Every studio that adopted generative
tooling in a delivery path added a clearance step, and three added a named
role for it.
