# Commissioning survey extract

Date: 2025-02-03
Source: survey of 312 commissioning editors and art buyers

Commissioning behaviour has shifted at the entry level and held steady above
it. Respondents reported cutting the volume of low-complexity briefs -- spot
illustration, stock-adjacent photography, short functional copy -- while
maintaining or increasing spend on work that carries a named author or a
distinctive style.

Where budgets moved, they moved toward fewer, larger commissions. Editors
described this as consolidating around people whose work they could not
substitute, and treating the substitutable tier as a tooling problem.

A recurring theme was disclosure. Just over half of respondents said their
organisation now asks suppliers to declare generative involvement in delivered
work, but only a minority said they had any way of verifying the declaration.

Respondents were notably split on quality. Those commissioning functional
content reported no measurable drop in audience response; those commissioning
narrative or editorial work reported that generated drafts cost more to fix
than to commission fresh.
